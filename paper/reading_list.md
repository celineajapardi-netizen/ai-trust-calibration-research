# Reading list — starting points for the Literature Review

> **Verify every reference before you cite it.** These are papers I believe exist with these
> details, but you must look each one up (Google Scholar is easiest), confirm the title, authors,
> year and venue, and read at least the abstract. Never cite something you haven't checked —
> an examiner may ask you about any reference in your list. Add the DOI/URL once found.

## Foundations: trust in automation (read these first)

1. **Lee, J. D., & See, K. A. (2004).** Trust in automation: Designing for appropriate reliance.
   *Human Factors, 46*(1), 50–80.
   → *The* paper on trust calibration. Defines calibration, over-trust, under-trust. Long, but
   read the first ten pages.

2. **Parasuraman, R., & Riley, V. (1997).** Humans and automation: Use, misuse, disuse, abuse.
   *Human Factors, 39*(2), 230–253.
   → "Misuse" = over-reliance; "disuse" = under-reliance. Good vocabulary for your Discussion.

3. **Dzindolet, M. T., Peterson, S. A., Pomranky, R. A., Pierce, L. G., & Beck, H. P. (2003).**
   The role of trust in automation reliance. *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies,
   58*(6), 697–718.
   → People trust automation until they see it make an error; explanations restore trust.

## Algorithm aversion vs appreciation

4. **Dietvorst, B. J., Simmons, J. P., & Massey, C. (2015).** Algorithm aversion: People
   erroneously avoid algorithms after seeing them err. *Journal of Experimental Psychology:
   General, 144*(1), 114–126.

5. **Logg, J. M., Minson, J. A., & Moore, D. A. (2019).** Algorithm appreciation: People prefer
   algorithmic to human judgment. *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 151*,
   90–103.
   → Read 4 and 5 together: they appear to contradict each other; the difference is context.

## Confidence displays, explanations and over-reliance (closest to your study)

6. **Zhang, Y., Liao, Q. V., & Bellamy, R. K. E. (2020).** Effect of confidence and explanation
   on accuracy and trust calibration in AI-assisted decision making. *Proceedings of the ACM
   Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency (FAccT '20)*.
   → Almost your design, with an income-prediction task. Confidence scores helped calibration;
   explanations did not. Compare your results with theirs directly.

7. **Bansal, G., Wu, T., Zhou, J., Fok, R., Nushi, B., Kamar, E., Ribeiro, M. T., & Weld, D. S.
   (2021).** Does the whole exceed its parts? The effect of AI explanations on complementary team
   performance. *Proceedings of CHI '21*.
   → Explanations increased reliance on the AI **whether or not it was right**. Directly relevant
   to your Conditions C/D.

8. **Buçinca, Z., Malaya, L. B., & Gajos, K. Z. (2021).** To trust or to think: Cognitive forcing
   functions can reduce overreliance on AI in AI-assisted decision-making. *Proceedings of the ACM
   on Human-Computer Interaction, 5*(CSCW1).
   → Ways to make people think before accepting AI output.

9. **Vasconcelos, H., Jörke, M., Grunde-McLaughlin, M., Gerstenberg, T., Bernstein, M. S., &
   Krishna, R. (2023).** Explanations can reduce overreliance on AI systems during
   decision-making. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, 7*(CSCW1).
   → Explanations help only when checking them is easier than doing the task yourself.

## Uncertainty expressed by language models (most recent)

10. **Kim, S. S. Y., Liao, Q. V., Vorvoreanu, M., Ballard, S., & Vaughan, J. W. (2024).**
    "I'm not sure, but…": Examining the impact of large language models' uncertainty expression
    on user reliance and trust. *Proceedings of FAccT '24*.
    → First-person uncertainty ("I'm not sure") reduced over-reliance on wrong answers.

11. **Zhou, K., Jurafsky, D., & Hashimoto, T. (2023).** Navigating the grey area: How expressions
    of uncertainty and overconfidence affect language models. *Proceedings of EMNLP 2023*.

12. **Steyvers, M., Tejeda, H., Kumar, A., Belem, C., Karny, S., Hu, X., Mayer, L., & Smyth, P.
    (2025).** What large language models know and what people think they know. *Nature Machine
    Intelligence*.
    → People over-estimate how accurate an LLM is unless its uncertainty is shown clearly.

## How to read efficiently

- Read the **abstract**, the **figures**, and the **first paragraph of the Discussion**. That's
  enough for most references.
- For 1, 6 and 7 read more carefully — they're the ones you'll compare your results with.
- Keep a one-line note per paper: *what they did, what they found, how it relates to my study*.
  Those notes basically become your Section 2.
- Google Scholar → "Cited by" on paper 6 is a good way to find newer studies.
